# Character And Brand Controls

## Character Consistency Package

For every recurring identifiable person or avatar, record:

- approved name, role, apparent age, face, hair, skin tone, facial hair, build, expressions, and distinguishing features;
- fixed identity traits and explicitly changeable wardrobe, pose, location, framing, and lighting;
- front-facing reference plus three-quarter, side, and full-body references when available;
- approved master image, avatar ID, character ID, image ID, seed, and accepted prompt where supported;
- usage consent, commercial rights, restrictions, and secure source location;
- provider-specific inputs and accepted output examples.

If no exact identity exists, create low-cost still-image candidates first and obtain approval for one master identity before video generation. Do not substitute a new face mid-production.

## Scene Enforcement

- Attach the same relevant package or saved avatar ID to every related request.
- Repeat fixed identity traits in provider prompts when the provider does not preserve project context.
- Permit deliberate changes only when the brief labels them changeable.
- Use stable specialist avatar tools for recurring lip-synced presenters; use general video models for cinematic movement when exact lip-sync matters less.
- Reject a result when the face, age, body, logo patch, required wardrobe, or other fixed trait changes materially.

## Brand And Text

- Use original logo files and approved font assets; never redraw a logo with a generative model.
- Apply captions, titles, calls to action, prices, phone numbers, URLs, and legal text in the editable composition layer.
- Keep captions and required graphics inside channel-safe areas.
- Record stock, client, owned, and generated asset provenance separately.
- Do not use unlicensed music, footage, fonts, likenesses, or client assets outside their permitted use.
